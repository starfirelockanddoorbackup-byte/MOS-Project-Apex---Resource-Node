// Emergency Pantry Supply Guidelines

  • Staples: White rice, dried beans, rolled oats, iodized salt, honey, peanut butter, and canned proteins (tuna, chicken).
  • Shelf-Life Rotation: Prioritize first-in, first-out (FIFO). Store in sealed Mylar bags with oxygen absorbers in cool, dry conditions.

// Foraging & Cultivation Notes

  • Wild Edibles (Regional): Focus on high-confidence wild greens (dandelion, purslane, amaranth). Avoid lookalikes.
  • Microgreens & Sprouting: Keep small jars of mung beans or alfalfa seeds for rapid, high-nutrient vitamin production indoors with minimal water.

Water Reserves & Filtration

Hydration Directive: "The best container for water is your body." Never arbitrarily ration emergency drinking water below baseline physiological needs during high heat or stress to prevent acute renal shutdown.

// Rudimentary Rainwater Filtration Device Design

  • Stage 1 (Coarse Sediment Trap): Clean cloth or fine mesh screen across catchment funnel to catch leaves and debris.
  • Stage 2 (Multi-Layer Gravity Column): Inverted cut plastic bottle layered with washed coarse gravel (bottom), fine sand, activated charcoal (removes chemical impurities/odors), and cotton/cloth at the spout.
  • Stage 3 (Pathogen Sterilization): Rolling boil for a minimum of 1 minute (3 minutes above 6,500 ft) or chemical disinfection (calcium hypochlorite / chlorine dioxide) prior to consumption.

Fuel, Generator & Solar Power

Solar Power Bank Recommendation: Stockpile rugged, high-capacity lithium iron phosphate (LiFePO4) portable solar power banks (e.g., 300Wh–1000Wh stations paired with foldable 100W–200W solar panels). They provide silent, zero-emission charging for communications, medical devices, and LED lighting without fuel dependency or carbon monoxide hazards.
Carbon Monoxide Hazard: Run fuel generators strictly outdoors, at least 20 feet away from structures, windows, and air intakes.

Clinical & TCCC Protocol Library

Advanced tactical protocols, prescription buffers, and austere medical references.

// 90-Day Prescription Buffer Directive

Maintain a strict 90-day supply buffer for all essential maintenance medications. Coordinate proactively with your primary care physician and insurance provider ahead of contingencies to secure an authorized emergency 90-day override supply.

// M - Massive Hemorrhage (M.A.R.C.H. P.A.W.S.)

Apply windlass tourniquet high and tight on extremities. For junctional sites (groin/axilla), pack wound firmly with hemostatic gauze and maintain direct pressure for a minimum of 3 minutes. Re-evaluate distal pulses.

// A - Airway Management

Clear blood, vomit, and debris manually. Insert Nasopharyngeal Airway (NPA) lubricated with water-soluble jelly. Place unconscious casualties in the recovery position. Prepare for surgical cricothyroidotomy if maxillofacial trauma obstructs airway.

// R - Respiration & Tension Pneumothorax

Apply vented chest seals on exhalation for sucking chest wounds. Monitor continuously for tension pneumothorax (absent breath sounds, progressive respiratory distress, tracheal deviation, hemodynamic collapse); perform immediate needle decompression at 2nd intercostal space mid-clavicular line or 5th intercostal anterior axillary line.

// C - Circulation & Shock Management

Re-assess tourniquets and pressure dressings. Establish IV or IO access if indicated for fluid resuscitation or blood product administration. Administer tranexamic acid (TXA) within 3 hours of injury if severe internal hemorrhage or hemorrhagic shock is suspected.

// H - Hypothermia & Head Injuries

Wrap casualty in active or passive warming blankets to prevent the lethal triad of trauma (hypothermia, acidosis, coagulopathy). Monitor level of consciousness (AVPU / GCS) and pupillary response for traumatic brain injury (TBI).

// P.A.W.S. - Pain, Antibiotics, Wound, Splint

P - Pain Management: Administer oral analgesics (Meloxicam / Tylenol) or Ketamine if trained/indicated.
A - Antibiotics: Initiate broad-spectrum oral prophylaxis (Moxifloxacin) for open wounds.
W - Wounds & Burns: Dress burns with clean dry sterile dressings; immobilize fractures.
S - Splinting & Documentation: Secure fractures and record all interventions on TCCC Card (DD Form 1380).

// Pharmaceuticals & Sensitive Populations

Pediatric Adjustments: Never administer adult weight-based dosages without verified pediatric dosing charts or Broselow tape metrics. Shake liquid suspensions thoroughly after thermal storage swings.
Geriatric Precautions: Adjust NSAID and antibiotic dosing for reduced baseline renal clearance.
Thermal Preservation: Store pharmaceuticals within 59°F–86°F. Protect insulin and liquid biologics from grid-down summer heat spikes using insulated containers or subterranean caches.
