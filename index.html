// Emergency Pantry Supply Guidelines

  • Staples: White rice, black beans, rolled oats, peanut butter, and canned proteins (tuna, chicken).
  • Shelf-Life Rotation: Prioritize first-in, first-out (FIFO). Store in sealed containers in cool, dry conditions.

Water Reserves & Filtration

Hydration Directive: "The best container for water is your body." Never arbitrarily ration emergency drinking water below baseline physiological needs during high heat or stress to prevent acute renal shutdown.

// Rudimentary Rainwater Filtration Device Design

  • Stage 1 (Coarse Sediment Trap): Clean cloth or fine mesh screen across catchment funnel to catch leaves and debris.
  • Stage 2 (Multi-Layer Gravity Column): Inverted cut plastic bottle layered with washed coarse gravel (bottom), fine sand, activated charcoal (removes chemical impurities/odors), and cotton/cloth at the spout.
  • Stage 3 (Pathogen Sterilization): Rolling boil for a minimum of 1 minute (3 minutes above 6,500 ft) or chemical disinfection prior to consumption.

Fuel, Generator & Solar Power

Solar Power Bank Recommendation: Stockpile rugged, high-capacity lithium iron phosphate (LiFePO4) portable solar power banks paired with foldable 100W–200W solar panels for silent, zero-emission charging without fuel dependency.
Carbon Monoxide Hazard: Run fuel generators strictly outdoors, at least 20 feet away from structures, windows, and air intakes.

Clinical & TCCC Protocol Library

Advanced tactical protocols, prescription buffers, and austere medical references.

// 90-Day Prescription Buffer Directive

Maintain a strict 90-day supply buffer for all essential maintenance medications. Coordinate proactively with your primary care physician ahead of contingencies.

// M - Massive Hemorrhage (M.A.R.C.H. P.A.W.S.)

Apply windlass tourniquet high and tight on extremities. Pack junctional wounds firmly with hemostatic gauze and maintain direct pressure for 3+ minutes.

// A - Airway Management

Clear blood, vomit, and debris manually. Insert Nasopharyngeal Airway (NPA) lubricated with water-soluble jelly. Place unconscious casualties in recovery position.

// R - Respiration & Tension Pneumothorax

Apply vented chest seals on exhalation for sucking chest wounds. Perform immediate needle decompression for tension pneumothorax.

// C - Circulation & Shock Management

Re-assess tourniquets. Administer tranexamic acid (TXA) within 3 hours if severe internal hemorrhage or hemorrhagic shock is suspected.

// H - Hypothermia & Head Injuries

Wrap casualty in warming blankets to prevent the lethal triad of trauma. Monitor level of consciousness and pupillary response.

// P.A.W.S. - Pain, Antibiotics, Wound, Splint

P/A/W/S: Administer oral analgesics and broad-spectrum antibiotics for open wounds, dress burns with sterile dressings, and secure fractures.

General Survival & Community Resilience

Practical guidance for sheltering in place, maintaining household calm, and fostering mutual aid.

// Sheltering in Place: Practical Fundamentals

  • Establish a Safe Inner Room: Choose an interior room with no windows or minimal glass, ideally on the lowest safe floor.
  • Keep Calm & Explain to Children: Children take cues directly from adults. Keep explanations simple, honest, and reassuring—turn preparations into a team mission.
  • Maintain Routine: Keep regular daily schedules, sleep cycles, and shared meals to stabilize household morale.

// Community & Mutual Aid

  • Fear Isolation, Not Neighbors: Crises test social fabric. A resilient neighborhood relies on open communication and mutual check-ins.
  • Share Strengths: Lean on each other's diverse capabilities—medical knowledge, mechanical skills, or calm leadership.
  • Lifting Each Other Up: True security is collective. When neighbors help neighbors, entire communities weather crises safely together.
